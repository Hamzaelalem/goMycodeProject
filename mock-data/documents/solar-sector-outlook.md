# Solar & Renewable Energy Sector Outlook — 2026

## Sector Overview
The global solar energy market reached $380 billion in 2025, growing at 14.2% CAGR. Utility-scale solar (>10MW) now accounts for 68% of new capacity additions globally. Sub-Saharan Africa and Southeast Asia represent the highest-growth emerging market segments.

## Global Market Dynamics
- Levelised Cost of Energy (LCOE): Solar now $28/MWh globally (down 92% since 2010)
- Module prices: $0.18/Watt (Q1 2026) — stabilized after Q4 2025 correction
- Battery storage attachment rate: 43% of new utility-scale projects
- Interconnection queue backlog: 18-24 months average (US, Europe)

## Regional Analysis

### Europe — Iberian Peninsula
Spain and Portugal combined installed 12.4 GW in 2025. Regulatory environment: stable, EU Green Deal providing €8.4B in solar subsidies. Offtake: long-term PPAs averaging €48/MWh (15-year). Land availability in Extremadura and Alentejo remains strong. Grid connection fees recently standardized. Iberia is the highest-irradiation region in Europe (2,000-2,800 kWh/m²/year).

CLIENT Exposure: Helios Solar Roll-up (Spain/Portugal) — Rank #1 recommendation, 94% confidence, $320M capital, 16.8% IRR projected.

### North America — Texas
ERCOT grid has absorbed 28 GW of solar capacity. Merchant market exposure significant — no regulated offtake requirement. Basis risk between ERCOT West and Hub pricing up to $8/MWh. Investment Tax Credit (ITC): 30% base + 10% domestic content bonus. Texas permitting: 6-9 months average.

CLIENT Exposure: Bluewater Utility-Scale Solar (Texas) — Rank #2 recommendation, 90% confidence, $210M capital, 15.1% IRR projected.

### Sub-Saharan Africa
Kenya, Ghana, Nigeria, South Africa leading solar deployment. Blended finance structures (DFI + private) required for most utility-scale projects. Currency risk mitigation via MIGA/DFI guarantees. Average IRR achievable: 14-19% (USD-hedged).

## ESG Considerations
- Carbon credits: 1 tonne CO₂ avoided per ~1.5 MWh generated
- Community energy access: Projects above 10MW expected to fund local electrification
- Land use conflicts: Key ESG risk in Sub-Saharan Africa
- Water intensity: Minimal (dry cooling standard for solar PV)

## Risk Factors
- Interest rate sensitivity: Each 100bps increase reduces IRR by approximately 1.2%
- Module supply chain: 80% concentrated in China — tariff risk elevated
- Grid curtailment risk: Growing in mature markets (Spain 4.2%, Texas 8.1%)
- FX risk: Significant for African projects with USD-denominated debt

## Key Performance Indicators
- LCOE target: < $35/MWh for bankability
- Capacity factor threshold: > 22% (utility-scale)
- Debt service coverage ratio minimum: 1.35x
- Construction timeline: 18-30 months (utility-scale)

## Outlook
Solar remains the fastest-growing energy technology globally. Pipeline visibility through 2030 is strong. The primary risk is execution: land, grid connection, and supply chain management are the key differentiators between successful and underperforming assets.
