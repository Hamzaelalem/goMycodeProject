# Banking & Financial Services Sector Outlook — 2026

## Sector Overview
Emerging market banking continues to outperform developed market peers on growth metrics. Africa's banking sector is experiencing a fintech-driven transformation, with mobile money, digital lending, and agency banking expanding financial inclusion. CLIENT's portfolio banking exposure is concentrated in East Africa and the Gulf region.

## Key Metrics — African Banking
- Return on Equity (ROE) sector average: 18.2% (East Africa), 14.1% (West Africa)
- Non-Performing Loan (NPL) ratio: 14.2% Kenya, 8.1% Uganda, 6.4% Rwanda
- Capital Adequacy Ratio: Sector average 18.4% (above Basel III minimum of 10.5%)
- Net Interest Margin: 7.8% average (significantly above developed market 2-3%)
- Digital transaction share: 76% of all transactions (East Africa)

## Country Deep-Dives

### Kenya — Banking Sector
Big 4: Equity Bank, KCB, Co-operative Bank, ABSA Kenya. Equity Bank market cap: KES 142B. KCB Group (largest by assets): KES 1.8T total assets. Growth drivers: SME lending, regional expansion (DRC, Rwanda, Uganda), mobile banking. Key risk: Elevated NPL ratio (14.2%) driven by personal loans. Capital adequacy remains strong.

CLIENT portfolio company: Equity Bank — under monitoring for regional expansion opportunity.

### Gulf Cooperation Council — Islamic Finance
GCC Islamic banking assets: $1.2 trillion (2025). Saudi Arabia Vision 2030 driving private sector credit growth. UAE attracting international capital through ADGM and DIFC free zones. Key products: Murabaha, Sukuk, Ijara. Sharia-compliant structuring adds 15-25bps cost premium.

CLIENT Exposure: 3 banking-sector recommendations (East Africa ×2, GCC ×1).

## ESG Considerations — Banking
- Financial inclusion index: Primary social KPI — % of adults with formal banking access
- Green loan portfolio percentage: Threshold >15% for ESG certification
- Employee diversity: Gender parity in management a growing investor requirement
- NPL ratio: Governance indicator — reflects credit discipline
- Capital adequacy: Governance indicator — reflects regulatory compliance culture
- Paper usage: Environmental KPI — digital-first operations preferred

## Risk Factors
- Credit risk: Elevated NPLs in post-pandemic recovery phase
- Currency risk: Local currency lending funded with USD wholesale borrowing creates mismatch
- Regulatory risk: Medium — CBK, Bank of Uganda maintaining active oversight
- Interest rate risk: Rising rates compressing fixed-rate loan book margins
- Digital disruption: Fintech competition intensifying (M-Pesa, OPay, Wave)

## Sector Outlook
African banking offers a compelling risk-adjusted return profile for patient capital. The combination of high NIMs, growing middle class, and digital infrastructure buildout creates a durable growth runway. Key selection criteria: strong governance (NPL discipline), digital capability, and regional diversification.
