# Oil & Gas Sector Outlook — 2026

## Sector Overview
Global oil demand reached 103.2 mb/d in 2025 (IEA). OPEC+ maintains disciplined production management. Energy transition is creating bifurcated market dynamics: mature basin decline vs. high-growth emerging market demand. Brent crude Q1 2026 average: $89/bbl.

## Macro Price Drivers
- OPEC+ production cuts: 2.2 mb/d ongoing through Q3 2026
- US shale breakeven: $52-58/bbl (Permian Basin)
- Demand growth concentrated in Asia (India +8%, China +2%)
- Energy transition reducing long-term demand ceiling estimates
- Geopolitical premium: $4-8/bbl (Middle East tensions)

## Regional Analysis

### North Africa — Algeria & Libya
Algeria (Sonatrach) maintaining stable output at 1.8 mb/d. Gas exports to Europe growing via Medgaz pipeline. Libya production volatile (900k-1.1 mb/d range) due to political instability. Regulatory framework improving for foreign direct investment in Algeria.

### West Africa — Nigeria & Senegal
Nigeria production recovery to 1.55 mb/d (from 1.2 mb/d in 2023). Dangote Refinery (650k bpd capacity) operational — reducing refined product import dependence. Senegal first oil: Sangomar field producing 100k bpd from Q2 2025. New producer with stable regulatory framework.

CLIENT Exposure: 2 Oil & Gas recommendations in current pipeline targeting North Africa and West Africa.

## ESG Considerations
- Flaring intensity: Key metric — Nigeria average 4.2 scf/bbl (industry target <1.0)
- Methane leakage rate: OGMP 2.0 Level 5 reporting required for institutional investors
- Spill incidents: Zero-tolerance requirement for ESG-compliant portfolios
- Energy transition alignment: "Managed decline" assets vs. long-life production
- Local employment percentage: Minimum 60% local content required (Nigeria regulations)

## Financial Metrics
- Project IRR threshold: >15% (risked) for development assets
- Breakeven oil price threshold: <$65/bbl for portfolio qualification
- Reserve life index target: >8 years for development stage assets
- Royalty rates: 10-20% (Nigeria), 16.25% (Algeria), 12.5% (Senegal)

## Risk Factors
- Oil price risk: High sensitivity — each $10/bbl change = ±2-3% IRR impact
- Regulatory risk: Medium-high in Nigeria (PIB implementation ongoing)
- Currency risk: USD-denominated revenues provide natural hedge
- Political risk: High in Libya, Medium in Nigeria, Low in Algeria and Senegal
- Stranded asset risk: Growing for assets with production life >15 years

## Sector Outlook
The oil and gas sector faces a structural tension between near-term capital allocation pressure (investors demanding returns) and long-term energy transition risk. CLIENT's exposure is limited to assets with <10-year production horizons and strong ESG credentials to manage transition risk.
