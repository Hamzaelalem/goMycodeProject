# Kenya Investment Brief — Q1 2026

## Country Overview
Kenya is East Africa's largest economy with a GDP of approximately $118 billion (2025 estimate). The country maintains a stable democratic government and has positioned itself as a regional technology and financial hub. Nairobi serves as the primary financial center for the East African Community.

## Macroeconomic Indicators
- GDP Growth: 5.2% (2025 actual), 5.6% forecast (2026)
- Inflation: 6.8% (March 2026)
- Exchange Rate: KES 128.4 / USD (Q1 2026 average)
- Benchmark Interest Rate: 13.0% (Central Bank of Kenya)
- Current Account Deficit: -5.1% of GDP
- Public Debt: 67.3% of GDP

## Investment Climate
Kenya offers one of the most business-friendly environments in Sub-Saharan Africa. The World Bank Doing Business Index ranks Kenya 56th globally. Key advantages include:
- Young, English-speaking workforce (median age: 20)
- Nairobi as a regional HQ hub for multinationals
- M-Pesa mobile money ecosystem enabling financial inclusion
- Special Economic Zones in Mombasa and Nairobi

## Sector Opportunities

### Solar & Renewable Energy
Kenya targets 100% renewable electricity by 2030. Current renewable mix is 92% (geothermal, hydro, wind). Solar capacity is underdeveloped relative to irradiation levels. Offtake agreements backed by Kenya Power with government guarantee available for projects above 5MW. Feed-in tariff: $0.072/kWh for solar.

### Banking & Financial Services
Equity Bank, KCB, and Co-operative Bank dominate. Fintech penetration rate is 83% (highest in Africa). Regulatory environment: Central Bank of Kenya maintains Basel III compliance requirements. NPL ratio sector average: 14.2% (elevated post-COVID). Digital lending regulation tightened in 2024.

### Healthcare
Public health spending: 4.9% of GDP. Private hospital bed density: 1.4 per 1,000 population (significant gap). Medical tourism from Uganda, Tanzania, and South Sudan growing at 18% CAGR. Kenya Medical Supplies Authority governs pharmaceutical procurement.

## Risk Factors
- Currency Risk: KES depreciated 12% against USD in 2024
- Political Risk: Low (stable post-2022 election)
- Regulatory Risk: Medium (ongoing energy sector reform)
- Debt Sustainability: Elevated (IMF programme in progress)
